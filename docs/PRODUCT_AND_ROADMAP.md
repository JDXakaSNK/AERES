# AERES — concept, market, roadmap

## Vision
A regional network of modular aerial platforms that could, if technically and legally validated, help professional responders with three classes of time-sensitive logistics:

1. **AED assistance** — delivery of a certified automated external defibrillator to a trained or remotely instructed bystander (requires independently validated responder workflows).
2. **Emergency visibility** — a high-visibility aerial lighting and observation platform for scene assessment (must not distract drivers or replace established road safety measures).
3. **Medical express** — temperature-monitored transport of suitable medical supplies (subject to chain-of-custody and qualified oversight).

The startup's proposed innovation would be mission orchestration, modular payload planning and validated, resilient operations — **not** the assumption that all tasks may be flown automatically or that a prototype can save lives.

## Customers to interview (hypotheses)
Municipal emergency planners, rescue organizations, event operators, hospital logistics teams, industrial site safety managers and road infrastructure managers. All require tailored permissions, operating procedures and commercial agreements.

## Business model hypotheses
- Annual B2B support/monitoring software fee for an authorized fleet operator
- Pilot study with an academic/industrial partner
- Service contract for logistics and maintenance, subject to approvals
- Training/simulation platform for emergency planning teams

**Do not treat revenue ranges or growth forecasts as validated.** Interview customers and quantify costs, approval timelines, staffing, liability, operations and insurance before pitching ROI.

## Product delivery milestones
- **M0 — simulation foundation:** local FastAPI + React app, synthetic incidents, manual decision gate, tests (delivered).
- **M1 — product-grade simulator:** persistent fixtures, reliable event replay, better virtual geography, accessibility, automated frontend testing.
- **M2 — field feasibility studies:** requirements interviews, equipment assessment, privacy threat modeling, independent safety engineering.
- **M3 — controlled demonstrations:** only after relevant authorizations, certified partners and formal procedures; separate software/hardware workstream.
- **M4 — network concept:** integration studies with appropriate public/private dispatch authorities, if agreements permit.

## Product analytics (simulation versus future field proof)
MVP measures technical usability, backend reliability and virtual workflow completion, **not medical benefit**. Real-world future study metrics require qualified study design: response-time distribution, deployment success under weather and airspace constraints, operations cost, human factors, false deployments and patient outcome methodology.

## Startup validation checklist
- Interview at least 10 stakeholders across hospitals, rescue and industrial safety before buying hardware.
- Map actual operational geography and likely authorization class with specialist support.
- Estimate total operating cost including staff, pads/batteries, inspections, connectivity, insurance, logistics and standby weather limits.
- Validate stakeholder ownership of dispatch decisions and device custody.
- Create a formal security/privacy impact assessment if live video, geolocation or medical data is considered.
